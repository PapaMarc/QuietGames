The Seder plate (Hebrew: *ke'arah* קְעָרָה)

•	Matzah

•	*zeroa* (shankbone): A piece of roasted meat represents the lamb that was the special paschal sacrifice on the eve of the exodus from Egypt, and annually on the afternoon before Passover in the Holy Temple.

•	Egg: The Aramaic word for “egg” is *bei’ah*, which is similar to the Aramaic word for “desire,” expressing that this was the night when G-d desired to redeem us.

•	bitter herbs: Bitter herbs (*maror  https://www.chabad.org/holidays/passover/pesach\_cdo/aid/1749/jewish/What-Is-Maror.htm*) remind us of the bitterness of the slavery of our forefathers in Egypt. Fresh grated horseradish, and romaine lettuce (or endives), are the most common choices.

•	*charoset* paste: A mixture of apples, pears, nuts and wine, which resembles the mortar and brick made by the Jews when they toiled for Pharaoh.

•	*karpas* vegetable-- Hebrew letters of karpas can be arranged to spell the word *perech* plus the letter *samech*. *Perech* means backbreaking work, and *samech* ( https://www.chabad.org/library/article\_cdo/aid/137087/jewish/Samech.htm ) is numerically equivalent to 60, referring to 60 myriads, equaling 600,000, which was the number of Jewish males over 20 years of age who were enslaved in Egypt.
